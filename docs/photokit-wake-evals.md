# When does Photos wake the In Process extension?

**The open product question is whether adding an existing still to the connected album is sufficient for unattended publication.** A wake means the extension logged entry into `processJobs()`. A scheduler submission means Photos requested execution. A host scan, a Pending label, or a new Moment alone cannot tell us when the extension woke.

## Physical-device experiment matrix

All rows use the baseline and scoring below. Each action starts its own clock. Every row needs the physical iPhone for its wake verdict, because the upload extension does not run on Simulator. **Pending control.** Complete row 10 setup, confirm exactly one pending operation with no registered job, background the host, and make no further change for 15 minutes. Repeat from that same starting state with a fresh fixture for rows 8 and 9. If preparing the pending item already scheduled the runner, record it before the intervention. Even a successful upload then gives limited evidence about which event prompted execution.

**Other conditions.** Resource Upload Test Mode, debugger attachment, Wi-Fi, external power, device lock and Mirroring can affect the experiment. Hold them constant within a comparison. Apple describes library monitoring after completion, further processing for outstanding work, and possible retries after a failure. Record retries or continued work as such. Apple leaves timing to the system. [Apple’s background upload guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/photokit/uploading-asset-resources-in-the-background)

## Run and score one row

1. Use the [known-state reset](core-workflow-evals.md) on a physical iPhone. Confirm the account, connected album, membership, permission, upload enablement, queue, outstanding jobs, last runner state and starting timeline IDs. Use a unique synthetic fixture, record its original bytes, and stage existing assets before the observation baseline. Reconcile any outstanding publication before resetting. Use Simulator for queue, deduplication and scoring logic; only the physical phone can supply wake evidence.
2. Run the positive controls with Test Mode on first. For normal behavior, repeat with it off and report those results separately. Record the switch, development launch and any runner confirmation, debugger detachment, actual host state, charging, network, Low Power Mode and Mirroring. Log unavailable conditions as unknown. Keep the host suspended during discovery trials and other synced devices quiet except for the specified action.
3. Start device logging before baseline and record the Mac and device clock offsets. For a local action, start the 15-minute observation clock when Photos confirms the change. For a remote action, record both end-to-end time from the iPad action and scheduling time from iPhone receipt. Poll the timeline read-only. Export retained app diagnostics after the cutoff and label that foreground boundary.
4. Record times for library change, scheduler submission and approval, extension entry, first detection and its process, job registration and completion, publication checkpoint and timeline readback. Record zero where absence can be verified, and mark missing evidence unknown. For publication require one new matching Moment and byte-identical media. A control with no eligible item requires zero unintended publications.
5. **Report three verdicts per run:** wake observed or unobserved within the measured interval; first discovery by extension, host, previously pending, or unknown; publication verified once, absent at cutoff, unexpected, or unknown. Mark early stops explicitly. Preserve late publication separately and list competing events. One temporal association cannot establish a guaranteed trigger. Repeat with independent fixtures before making a reliability claim.

**Observation window.** Each timed wait is 15 minutes, reduced from one hour at the user's request on November 18 so a full pass fits in one sitting. A 15-minute miss is weaker evidence than a one-hour miss. The one-hour product acceptance threshold in [the core plan](core-workflow-evals.md) is unchanged.

**Unattended procedure.** The goal is a run that needs no hands on the phone. The November 18 pass drove rows 1, 3, 5, 6, 10 and 11 from the Mac with iPhone Mirroring connected throughout. [Results](wake-eval-results-2026-11-18.md)

- **Fixtures:** take fresh app screenshots from the Simulator and keep their hashes. Drag each PNG from a Finder window onto the Photos library grid in iPhone Mirroring. That creates a new local library asset with known bytes.
- **Album membership:** in Photos open the photo, then the more-actions menu, Add To, and the In Process album under Recents.
- **Host state:** open In Process through Spotlight and return Home with the Mirroring Home command. Confirm the foreground and background events in the log; one Spotlight tap missed on November 18.
- **Evidence:** stream the device log from the Mac for the whole run. One filter keeps app and extension events; a second keeps Photos upload-runner and scheduler lines. Read the timeline back after each window and compare image bytes to the fixture hash.
- **Still needs a person:** row 4 (camera), rows 7–9 (iPad or another iCloud device), row 12 (unplugging power), row 13 (unlock, and Mirroring reconnection can ask for Mac authentication) and row 14 (a controlled test endpoint). Row 2 needs the album disconnected and emptied first.

**Recommended sequence.** Pair the quiet control with local import and existing membership (rows 1, 3, 6). Then compare pending-only, remote import and remote deletion under identical conditions (rows 8–10). That comparison addresses the latest ambiguous wake directly. Evaluate the transfer follow-ups using the jobs those runs already create.

An iCloud asset arrived at 14:48:28, Photos submitted the runner at 14:48:31 and the extension entered at 14:48:45. One byte-matched Moment appeared at 14:49:00. Publication took 46m 25s.
